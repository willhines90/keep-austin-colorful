Let's put the color back on 4th & Colorado.

Austin's rainbow crosswalk came up in July under a state order. Here is the happy part: San Antonio faced the very same order and painted its sidewalks instead. Two blocks of color, entirely legal, and it needed no council vote. Austin has every tool to do the same, a council that has said it wants to, and a neighborhood ready to help. Here is how, and how to ask in about five minutes.

The thing all of them worked out

Every city that got its color back found the same door, and it is a wide one. The state's order reaches the road surface. It does not reach sidewalks, steps, benches, bike racks, lamp posts or buildings, all of which belong to the city or to the people on the block.

San Antonio went furthest, and the reason is worth dwelling on: the sidewalk art didn't need a council vote, because the corridor had already been designated a Pride Cultural Heritage District the year before. The designation did the work. It turned a political fight into an administrative decision.

Austin has form here. Six Square, on the east side, was the first Black cultural heritage district in Texas. The city has done this before, and the West 4th corridor is a natural candidate for the same treatment.

A gentle word on how to help

Let the city hold the brush. Painting public property without authorization is criminal mischief under Texas law, and it would turn a warm story into a difficult one. The beauty of what San Antonio did is that it happened in daylight, with a ribbon, with the city proud to put its name on it. That is exactly what we are aiming for.

Aim at the sidewalk, not the road. Asking the city to repaint the crosswalk itself would put real road funding at risk, and it gives a reasonable person no way to say yes. The sidewalk is the ask because the sidewalk is ground the city can act on freely and gladly.

I'm not a lawyer and this isn't legal advice. It is a summary of what other Texas cities have done and been allowed to keep. Check anything consequential with someone qualified, or with the city.

A 30-second phone script

Council offices run on 512-978-21NN, where NN is the district number. Every number here links to the city page it came from.

“Hi, I'm a constituent in District [#]. I'm calling to ask the council member to support a rainbow sidewalk treatment at 4th and Colorado, the way San Antonio did on North Main, and to back a cultural heritage district designation for that corridor. Sidewalks aren't covered by the state's order. Could you log my support? Thank you.”

Staff log calls by position. You don't need to be eloquent or to win a debate. You need to be counted.
