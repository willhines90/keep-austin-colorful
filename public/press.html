Press kit

Everything you need to write about this quickly. Every figure is sourced, the illustrations are ours to license, and there is a human at the other end of the contact route at the bottom.

Keep Austin Colorful is a play on Keep Austin Weird. The color in the name is paint: the rainbow crosswalk removed from 4th and Colorado, and the sidewalk treatment that could lawfully replace it.

The story in one paragraph

In October 2025 Governor Abbott directed TxDOT to withhold road funding from Texas cities that kept "political" street art, following a similar federal directive. TxDOT denied every application to keep a rainbow crosswalk. Austin's, installed in 2021 at West 4th and Colorado in the heart of the city's LGBTQ+ district, was removed on 20 to 21 July 2026. But San Antonio, under the identical order, had already found a lawful route: it painted its sidewalks instead. The state order governs the roadway; sidewalks belong to the city. Two blocks of North Main Avenue, roughly $170,000, dedicated in March 2026, still there, and it needed no council vote because that corridor already held Pride Cultural Heritage District status. This campaign asks Austin to do the same.

The angle most coverage has missed

Most reporting has framed this as a loss. The more interesting story is jurisdictional: the state's authority stops at the kerb.

San Antonio's sidewalks did not merely survive; they required no council vote, because the corridor had been designated a Pride Cultural Heritage District in 2025, the first in Texas. The designation converted a political fight into an administrative decision. That is a replicable mechanism, and no Texas city other than San Antonio has used it yet.

Austin has precedent for the designation itself. Six Square, on the east side, was the first Black cultural heritage district in the state.

Quotes you can use

The state's order reaches the road. The sidewalk belongs to the city. San Antonio worked that out in January and had color back on the ground by March. Keep Austin Colorful
Austin did everything it was asked to do. It applied for an exemption, it complied when that was refused, and it put up a mural. The question now is simply what comes next, and there is a proven answer ninety miles down the road. Keep Austin Colorful
