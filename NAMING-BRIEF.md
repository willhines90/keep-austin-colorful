# Naming brief

Paste this into any AI tool, or hand it to a person. It is written to be used whole.

---

## The one-paragraph version

We need a name for a civic campaign in Austin, Texas. In July 2026 the state forced the city to scrape the rainbow crosswalk off the corner of West 4th and Colorado, in the heart of Austin's LGBTQ+ district. San Antonio, facing the identical order, painted its **sidewalks** rainbow instead, because the state's order governs roadways and sidewalks belong to the city. It cost about $170,000, has stood since March, survived a legal challenge, and required no council vote. The campaign asks Austin to do the same. The name chosen was **Keep Austin Colorful**.

---

## What the campaign actually is

A website plus a small identity. Not an organization, not a nonprofit, not a brand with a budget. One neighbor's project, built to be forked by other cities.

It asks the Austin City Council for two specific things:

1. A rainbow sidewalk treatment at 4th and Colorado, on San Antonio's model.
2. Designation of the West 4th corridor as an LGBTQ+ cultural heritage district, so it carries forward without depending on any single council.

## Who the name has to work on

Four audiences, in order of how much they matter:

- **Austin residents**, who need to understand it in one second and be willing to say it out loud to a neighbor.
- **City council members and their staff**, who need to feel invited rather than accused. This is the constraint most names fail.
- **Local press**, who need something that fits a headline and does not sound like a stunt.
- **People in other cities**, who might fork it. A name that generalizes is a small bonus, not a requirement.

## Tone

Warm, hopeful, a little playful, unmistakably Austin. Confident but never combative.

The single most important constraint: **the campaign's strategic advantage is that what it asks for is lawful and easy to say yes to.** San Antonio did it in daylight with a ribbon-cutting. If the name sounds angry, illicit, or like protest, it hands opponents the framing that this is agitation rather than civic participation, which is exactly the argument the campaign exists to defeat.

Aim for *municipal signage that happens to be joyful*. Not a picket sign.

## What the name should do

- Be sayable in a sentence: "I signed ______", "have you seen ______"
- Suggest color, return, or possibility
- Feel local without needing a local to explain it
- Work on a sticker, a placard, a council email subject line, and a domain

## What to avoid

- Anything that reads as a grievance, a demand, or a fight
- Generic Pride vocabulary that could belong to any city ("Pride Forward", "Rainbow Coalition")
- Puns that undercut the seriousness of a real municipal ask
- **"Keep Austin Weird" is a registered trademark** held by Outhouse Designs and it has been enforced. Riffing on a famous slogan is not itself infringement, but if the name goes on merchandise for sale it needs a real look. Non-commercial advocacy is much safer ground.
- Anything implying the city failed. The city complied under a genuine funding threat and has already responded with a mural and a historical marker.

## Raw material to draw on

**The corner:** West 4th and Colorado. Downtown. Austin's LGBTQ+ district. District 9.

**The mechanism:** the road belongs to the state, the sidewalk belongs to the city, and the curb is the line between them. This distinction is the entire argument and the richest source of ideas.

**Austin vocabulary:** the Violet Crown (the city's historic nickname, for the purple glow over the hills at dusk). Keep Austin Weird. Congress Avenue bats. Barton Springs. ATX. Sixth Street. Six Square, the first Black cultural heritage district in Texas.

**Texas vocabulary:** Don't Mess With Texas. Come and Take It. Six Flags Over Texas. Deep in the Heart. Y'all Means All. All hat, no cattle. The Lone Star.

**Timing:** Austin Pride 2026 falls on August 22, themed *Viva la Resistencia*. The city adopts its budget August 12 to 14.

**Numbers:** 5,330 people signed the petition. About 75 gathered on the corner the night after the removal. Three Texas cities have already put their color back.

## Names already considered

Keeping these here so you can go somewhere new, or tell us one of them was right.

**Name, as adopted:** Keep Austin Colorful

**Place-anchored:** Fourth & Forever · Meet Me at Fourth · Hold This Corner · Our Corner · The Corner Remembers

**Named for the legal move:** Take the Sidewalk · Off the Road · The Curb Line · City Property · Beside the Point

**Austin-native and wry:** Still Weird, Still Colorful · Out-Prided · Don't Mess With Our Corner · The San Antonio Standard · The Violet Crown

**Blunt:** Put It Back · Paint the Sidewalk · Give Us the Sidewalk

**Warm and defiant:** Still Here, Still Colorful · We Still Cross Here · Six Colors, One Corner · The Sidewalk Two-Step

Notes on why several were set aside: *Out-Prided* is funny and memorable but needling, and the audience includes the people being needled. *Put It Back* is chantable but slightly wrong, since the ask is a sidewalk rather than the crosswalk. *The Violet Crown* is the most beautiful and needs the most explanation.

## What we want back

For each suggestion:

1. The name
2. One line on why it works
3. How it sounds in a sentence a resident would actually say
4. Whether the `.org` is plausibly available

Give us fifteen to twenty across at least four distinct directions. We would rather see a few strange ones than twenty variations on the same idea.

## A test to apply before suggesting

Read the name out loud as if you were a council member's chief of staff who has just been handed a letter from a constituent. Does it make you want to help, or does it make you defensive?

If it makes you defensive, it fails, no matter how good it sounds on a tote bag.
